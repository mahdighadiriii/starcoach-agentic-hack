## 1. Audit findings

The initial repository contained one commit (`ac3edeb`) and extensive tracked and untracked work from prior sessions. That work was preserved. No commit, push, database deletion or interruption of the original strict processes occurred. The modular-monolith design remains intact. The unrelated dotone transfer view was not changed.

Confirmed defects corrected:

- Candle requests and analysis ran on the protection thread; REST fallbacks, account-display reads and webhook delivery could also stall it. The original exact 27-second duration was not independently reproduced; the blocking call graph was confirmed and deterministic stall tests now cover the corrected Shadow path.
- A reused book retained its original timestamp but could still drive simulated fills, exits and trailing stops. Metadata reads could initialize a misleading market freshness clock. New entries could remain allowed while an existing position had a stale mark.
- Paper balances survived restarts but paper orders did not. The prior restart test reused the same in-memory venue and therefore did not prove process recovery.
- Repeated polling could repeatedly consume the same displayed book for a paper order.
- The residual sweep inferred bot ownership from balances above a starting baseline, allowing later external deposits to be sold. SELLs lacked an independent ownership check. Sales without a position also could not be attributed to position P&L.
- A filled BUY recomputed its target from the fee-adjusted cost basis instead of the gross fill average, diverging from replay behavior. SELL fees in base were not subtracted from owned quantity.
- A fully sold position temporarily omitted its loss from allocation equity before the CLOSED transition. Existing-position planned stop risk omitted costs.
- Unknown order observations reused earlier known states; negative fee regressions and quote-only accounting adjustments were mishandled. Persisted CREATED intents were omitted from unresolved recovery.
- Open-order reads were limited to one page. Unexpected response shapes or HTTP 200 error envelopes could resemble empty successful results. Fee magnitudes were guessed to be percentages.
- Resampling accepted duplicate timestamps in place of missing minutes. Candle arrays were silently truncated by zip; forming/out-of-range candles, stale context and gaps needed stronger validation.
- Defaults allowed advisory gating; live preparation did not explicitly reject experimental/advisory profiles.
- Replay entries could fill through their submitted limit after a gap. Replay daily halts did not latch consistently; day boundaries used UTC, final liquidation costs were absent from the last equity mark, and initial-capital drawdown could be missed. One evaluation path divided allocation returns by 5M rather than 1M.
- Dashboard reads followed the status file into another profile database, opened SQLite connections without reliably closing them, omitted open-position fees from totals, did not label stale valuations adequately, and could report a paper balance refresh as a fresh exchange HTTP success.
- Run IDs could retain an old manifest after source/configuration changes. Manifests now preserve a new build segment without resetting the account ledger or risk state.
- A final real probe exposed a cleanup-order false failure: it requested its WebSocket evidence after stopping the feed. Evidence is now captured before shutdown, with a regression test and nonzero exit status on failed checks. Both the false-failure and corrected-success probe reports are retained.

Architectural concerns and limits:

- Shadow runtime orders, reconciliation, portfolio state and SQLite writes stay on one engine thread. Separate read-only pools cover protected books (2 workers), heartbeat books (1), candle/analysis work (1), account display (1), AI selection (1) and bounded alert delivery. There is only one in-flight job per symbol or decision batch; late decisions expire.
- Each background Wallex reader owns its connection pool and interface rebinding state, avoiding a close/rebuild race on a shared HTTP client.
- The live venue still performs network order submission, cancellation, balances and reconciliation synchronously. This is an explicit blocker to a live protection-timing guarantee; it was not enabled or represented as fixed.
- HTTP phase timeouts do not guarantee an absolute DNS/OS/disk deadline. An in-flight Python thread cannot be forcibly killed safely. A stuck worker cannot accumulate unbounded duplicate tasks or place orders, and stale data blocks affected entries.
- The file lock enforces one writer per database, not one writer across every possible database using the same exchange account. Software allocation is not exchange-level fund segregation.
- During an exchange outage, process outage or missing-price interval, stop/target execution is impossible to guarantee. No exchange-side stops were exercised. Daily-loss and drawdown thresholds are controls, not guaranteed loss ceilings through gaps.
- Below-minimum dust is retained and disclosed as a conservative write-off. Automatic unassigned liquidation was retired to preserve ownership and P&L attribution.

Donchian train/validation/test returns are +4.787% / +0.080% / −1.856%; only six validation and seven test trades. Its test average win/loss is about +3,294/−3,642 TMN and average hold about 17 hours. Scalp reversion has no winning test trades; average loss about 1,911 TMN and average hold 28.6 minutes. Momentum average win/loss is about +990/−2,571 TMN and average hold 24.8 minutes. These are modeled outcomes with small samples, not risk-adjusted performance guarantees.

At the assumed costs a round trip consumes roughly 96 bps before signal error. A nominal 120 bps gross scalp target leaves little margin, while a 70 bps stop becomes a much larger net loss. Runtime stops remain anchored to the signal level and targets use the executed gross price and configured R multiple, so effective percentages vary with the fill; the approximate cost comparison is not an exact payout guarantee. Validation remains negative for both scalp families even at zero fees (reversion −0.822%, momentum −2.193%). This argues against a sufficient gross edge in that sample.

Validation-only parameter perturbations did not establish robustness. Donchian ATR stop multipliers 2.5/3.5 gave −0.194% on seven trades / +0.259% on five. Scalp 0.5%/0.9% stop/target produced no fills because the unchanged cost gate rejected them; 1.0%/1.8% variants remained negative. None was promoted to production. Wider spread experiments and forward-window results are in the machine reports.

The Wallex dataset contains 161,098 genuine minute bars across eight markets over 14 days (approximately 99.8–99.9% coverage, 2–3 gaps per market). Incomplete minutes were not fabricated or forward-filled. The Wallex test split produced zero trades for all three families. There was only one reversion trade in train (about −0.186% allocation return); momentum had one candidate decision without a fill. This is insufficient for statistical validation, meaningful confidence bounds, or a positive-edge claim.

All three strategies require an upward context regime and the BTC reference filter; signals are intentionally suppressed in other regimes. EMA regime detection is lagging and its volatility input does not create a separate volatile regime. ATR, breakout, volume and prior-RSI conditions further reduce frequency. Current BTC RANGE conditions and delayed/incomplete last-minute candle publication explain HOLDs; Donchian also lacks complete context windows after real gaps. Repeated signals and portfolio limits are checked rather than forced through.

Limitations: static eight-market universe creates survivorship/selection risk; no point-in-time delisting universe; candles do not establish historic executable depth, spread or queue priority; replay uses synthetic unlimited depth and assumed costs; paper execution uses current displayed depth with a conservative fraction but cannot prove future liquidity. Real financing fees, current account fees and exact historical minimum/precision changes are not incorporated. No sufficiently large independent Wallex sample supports a risk-adjusted positive expectancy.

## 7. Remaining blockers

1. A real read-only Wallex key and the correct credit-account ID are required to validate authentication, account scope, free/locked balances, trading credit, active package, wallet link, fees, open orders and history. No credentials were invented or exposed.
2. Real order acceptance, partial-fill/cancel races, unknown outcomes, idempotency, fee currency, sub-account permissions and minimum-order enforcement remain unverified by design; this mission prohibited real order changes.
3. The synchronous live order/reconciliation path, missing exchange-side protective-stop validation and per-database rather than per-account locking prevent a live protection/reliability verdict.
4. Outages, process loss, publication delay and price gaps can prevent exits or cause missed decisions. Stale observations are explicitly rejected; software cannot guarantee a fill or a fixed maximum loss.
5. No credible repeatable positive edge has been established. The independent Wallex sample and trade count are too small; realistic historical liquidity, financing costs and independent out-of-sample evidence are still needed.
6. Supervised Shadow observation was short and produced no naturally occurring entry; a full real-data paper entry/exit and longer interruption/restart soak remain to be observed. Mocked execution coverage is not a substitute for that observation.
7. Legacy Shadow databases with orders but no durable venue snapshot are deliberately refused on restart; preserve them and use a new isolated evaluation DB. Existing original processes still have old loaded code.
8. Account allocation remains a software ceiling. Dedicated exchange fund isolation and verified credit-wallet connection settings are required before treating the remaining exchange funds as segregated.
